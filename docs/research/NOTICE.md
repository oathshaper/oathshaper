# Research, attribution and third-party rights

Last reviewed: 2026-10-22. This notice applies to Oathshaper's comparative research notes, interface studies and design references. It is project guidance, not a legal opinion or a determination that a particular use is lawful.

## Purpose and independence

These materials analyze published games and tools for criticism, commentary, research and education about game mechanics, accessibility and user experience. Oathshaper's proposed systems and examples are independent designs. References to other products identify the work being discussed; they do not indicate sponsorship, endorsement, partnership or affiliation.

Referenced names include BioWare, Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic, Jade Empire, Mass Effect, Dragon Age, Baldur's Gate, Final Fantasy, Chrono Trigger, Frozen Synapse, Pathfinder, Starfinder, Godot, Unreal Engine and Meshy. Names, marks, logos and other third-party material belong to their respective rights holders. An homage or dedication does not grant permission to use their branding or assets.

## Ideas, expression and fair use

U.S. copyright law distinguishes ideas, systems and methods of operation from the protected expression used to describe or depict them. That distinction supports studying interaction patterns; it does not authorize copying a game's code, artwork, music, dialogue, manuals, distinctive graphic treatment or other protected expression. See the [U.S. Copyright Office's Circular 33](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ33.pdf).

Where a limited quotation or reproduction is included for analysis, its purpose is criticism, commentary, teaching or research. Such purposes **may** qualify as fair use in the United States, but the assessment depends on the particular use, including its purpose and character, the nature of the source, the amount used, and market effects. Attribution, nonprofit intent, educational use, open publication or this notice alone does not establish fair use. Other jurisdictions have different exceptions. See the [Copyright Office's fair-use guidance](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/).

Trademark rights concern identification of commercial source and can raise separate issues. Descriptive references here do not grant a trademark license or establish clearance for a product name, logo or overall presentation. See the [USPTO's trademark overview](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/what-trademark). These notes are not a patent, trademark, trade-dress or freedom-to-operate review.

## Publication practices

- Prefer original analysis and links to official sources. Cite the edition, platform, relevant section and access date where practical. Distinguish verified behavior from inference and proposed Oathshaper behavior.
- Use only the quotation or visual excerpt needed to explain a specific observation. Identify its source, rights holder where known, analytical purpose and any relevant permission/license. Do not bundle manuals, extracted game data, ripped assets or substantial source passages as research attachments.
- Screenshots of Oathshaper and original study graphics must be labeled as such; they are not screenshots of the reference games. An Oathshaper capture can still contain third-party or generated assets requiring their own provenance review.
- Treat fonts, sound, character art, generated meshes, textures, rule text, example datasets and vendor API outputs separately. A source link, paid subscription or generated output does not by itself establish redistribution rights.
- Keep dependencies' license notices. Any use of licensed tabletop rule content must follow the applicable content license and attribution requirements; a comparison to a ruleset does not import that content or its license into this project.
- Before a public release, review each included third-party asset and any actual quotation/screenshot use. Obtain permission or qualified legal review where the basis is uncertain. This research notice is not that release clearance.
