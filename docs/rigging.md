# Character rig and animation contract

## Supplied Meshy player rig — 2026-10-28

Tessa uses the owner's 72-joint Meshy/Mixamo-style rig, with retargeted CC0 Quaternius Walk and Jog clips and a locally authored Idle. The original 8K albedo and 4K surface maps are preserved. [Initial Meshy integration](party-assets-20261028/README.md), [October 29 corrections](equipment-polish-20261029/README.md), and [library source, reproduction and teaching notes](../tools/rigging/sources/quaternius/README.md) describe the current player; older player-specific sections below are historical stand-in notes.

Build Tessa with `tools/rigging/import_party_assets.py -- ~/Downloads tessa` in Blender, then run `tools/rigging/retarget_library.py -- --install-walk` and `--install-run` to apply the library gaits. `rig_characters.py` builds only the four legacy NPC rigs; Mara and Ivo have fitted arm landmarks and hand weights. The character slot supports a Run clip, reads optional per-rig stride calibration from `.motion.json`, and accepts a `GroundContact` child for reliable placement of transformed skinned imports. Finger landmarks provide Tessa's weapon grip frame. Mara and Ivo retain single hand bones without separate finger articulation.

## Practice combat poses — 2026-10-22

The player rig was regenerated with `stabilize_coat_weights(..., player=True)`. A bounded lower-coat region transfers stray arm weights to its existing hips/spine anchors (278 vertices repaired). This prevents the skirt from stretching toward the hand during practice strikes. Original textures, source GLB, 19-bone skeleton and Idle/Walk authoring remain intact. `scripts/verify-rigged-glbs.py` verifies the weighted imports and required clips.

`combat_pose.gd` applies an additive SkeletonModifier3D after the locomotion animation, with skeleton-space rotations to account for imported bone roll. `weapon_presentation.gd` attaches original primitive weapon geometry to RHand. These are rough ready/strike/recovery poses; finger articulation, two-handed IK and production combat animation remain future work. [Pose capture](images/combat-pose-command-strike.png).

## Slow cadenced idle — 2026-10-21

The previous 1.3 mm breathing motion disappeared at gameplay distance. All five derived rigs now sway once every eight beats and breathe once every four beats. Each character has a slightly different tempo, approximately 58–69 beats/minute: an Idle loop takes 7.04–8.38 seconds in Godot. The torso leans gently, with the neck counterbalancing; hips and both feet stay planted. NPC Talk keeps this cadence while adding a small one-handed gesture. This is an authored animation rhythm, not a physiological simulation. Static-model fallbacks retain their small breathing scale; the sway is skeletal.

The Blender rebuild retained embedded texture payloads byte for byte and the player's exported Walk samples exactly. Godot sampled all five Idle cycles: maximum head displacement from the neutral pose was 23.39–24.30 mm, measured foot-joint drift was zero, and chest rotation changed by 1.08–1.14°. Loop seams, Talk cadence and feet are checked too. Judge the motion in the [actual exploration/dialogue camera recording](images/cadenced-idle-gameplay.mp4), alongside the [five-character study](images/quiet-character-breathing.mp4) and [verification notes](presentation-polish.md).

## Runtime contract

NPC slots now randomize the initial Idle phase and playback tempo, then drift smoothly between independently chosen tempos (0.9–1.1 times the authored rate, new targets every 4–9 seconds). Idle/Talk transitions preserve normalized phase instead of resetting the sway. This breaks the repeating crowd harmonics of fixed loops. `randomize_standing_motion` opts out; the player opts out to retain the tuned movement/idle cadence. `standing_motion_seed = 0` chooses a fresh per-instance seed; nonzero seeds make previews/tests reproducible. This presentation randomness never enters Rust narrative state or saves. Walk playback still follows movement speed.

`godot/scripts/character_model_slot.gd` is the visual swap point for the player, Mara, Ivo, civic clerk, and vendor. Put a preferred skinned scene at `rigged_model_path` and a static fallback at `model_path`, then run the Godot editor or `Godot --headless --path godot --import` before starting the demo. Each slot keeps its parent actor, collision, dialogue identity, and labels. A missing or invalid rig falls back to the static model; a missing, invalid, or empty static model leaves the primitive art visible.

For skeletal animation playback, the imported scene must contain a visible mesh bound to a `Skeleton3D` through a skin with vertex bone weights, plus an `AnimationPlayer` with bone tracks and an `Idle` clip. Add a looping `Walk` clip for a moving character. The slot accepts exact clip names and Godot library names such as `Locomotion/Idle` and `Locomotion/Walk`; other names can be set with the slot's `idle_animation` and `walk_animation` Inspector properties. If `Walk` is absent, the model keeps playing `Idle` while moving. The slot checks for a bound skin, nonzero weights, and required clip names before preferring a rigged scene; it does not judge deformation quality. The export verifier checks every vertex and nonconstant bone tracks.

The five original Meshy character GLBs each contain a single mesh and **no skin, skeleton, or animation clips**. Five derived GLBs provide skinned art for the player, Mara, Ivo, clerk, and vendor. The slot can give an unrigged visible mesh a small foot-anchored breathing idle and, for a moving `CharacterBody3D`, a rhythmic whole-body walk sway. This is presentation motion, not a walking rig: legs and arms do not articulate. `motion_mode()` reports `"rigged"`, `"procedural"`, or `"static"` for inspection. Set `procedural_motion = false` to keep a static model or let another script own its transforms. `grounded_idle_character.gd` aligns NPCs to the floor and supplies contact shadows; it skips whole-body breathing when a skeletal `Idle` plays.

The player slot reads horizontal `CharacterBody3D.velocity` from its parent and switches to `Walk` above `walk_speed_threshold` (default `0.15 m/s`). It scales walk playback to actor speed and returns to `Idle` when movement stops or dialogue disables the player. The demo opts into `reverse_walk_on_backpedal`, so the same in-place clip plays backward while the actor's `backpedaling` property is true. For unrigged art, velocity controls the procedural walk sway. Mara, Ivo, the clerk, and the vendor are stationary `Node3D` actors, so they use `Idle` and switch to `Talk` during their conversation. Animation is in Godot and does not enter the Rust narrative state or save. The slot loops clips at runtime, but does not retarget, blend skeletal clips, or drive root motion.

Author the model with its feet at local Y `0`, facing Godot's forward direction, or set `model_offset`, `model_scale`, and the slot's local yaw in the scene. The Meshy player front imports as local +Z, so its slot uses a 180° yaw while `player.gd` keeps -Z as actor forward. Animate the skeleton or mesh, not the imported scene root: root translation would fight the scene-owned player movement and grounding. Keep a body silhouette within the actor's existing collider until a dedicated collision pass. The optional visual model never supplies interaction range or narrative identity.

To get actual skinned walking, supply a rigged GLB with weighted vertices, a usable bone hierarchy, and `Idle`/`Walk` animations. The original five files cannot produce limb motion by simply enabling an option in Godot. Inspect any automated rigging result in a graphical build: coat hems, arm separation, and foot contact need particular attention.

## Rigging the current Meshy characters

The shortest route for the existing textured GLBs is Meshy's **Auto Rigging → Humanoid → Animate** flow. Check joint placement and deformation in the preview, add `Idle` and `Walk` for Emerald Corsair, then download all added animations in one GLB. The clerk and vendor need `Idle` and can have a gesture clip. Choose a walk **in place**: `player.gd` already moves the actor, and the slot does not consume root motion. Meshy documents [rigging](https://docs.meshy.ai/en/webapp/guides/3d-model/rigging), [animation](https://docs.meshy.ai/en/webapp/guides/animate), and [single-file GLB export](https://www.meshy.ai/tutorials/character-auto-rigging-workflow).

There is also a reproducible local Blender path. In Blender 5.0.1, import a **copy** of `player.glb` with **File → Import → glTF 2.0 → Merge Vertices** enabled. Add and align an armature, parent the mesh to it with **Armature Deform → With Automatic Weights**, then pose each joint and repair weights around the sleeves, coat, knees, and boots. Author looping `Idle` and in-place `Walk` actions, place each on its own NLA track, and export a GLB with skinning and animations. Blender documents [the glTF importer/exporter](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.0/addons/import_export/scene_gltf2.html) and [automatic-weight parenting](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.0/animation/armatures/skinning/parenting.html).

That local path is now scripted in [`tools/rigging/rig_characters.py`](../tools/rigging/rig_characters.py): run `blender -b --python-exit-code 1 --python tools/rigging/rig_characters.py -- player clerk vendor mara ivo` from the repository root. Blender can otherwise return shell exit 0 after a Python exception, so keep `--python-exit-code 1` in build commands. The script leaves the original GLBs untouched and exports five skinned derivatives to `godot/assets/meshes/rigged/`. Run `python3 scripts/verify-rigged-glbs.py` to check the exported skins, per-vertex weights, and nonconstant named bone clips before Godot import. Blender 5.0.1 was used for these exports; another version may change the glTF import/export API. The script uses generic bone placement and first-pass keyframes, so a character artist should still refine the weights and clips.

Mara's original mesh was modeled with spread legs and angled arms. Its derived `Idle` and `Talk` clips rotate the weighted upper arms, forearms, and upper legs inward, leaving the gloves beside her coat and narrowing the boots. A small keyed hip lift keeps the posed soles at the source mesh's floor height. This is a skeletal correction inside the animation clips, not a sideways scale of the whole model. The `Talk` clip adds a restrained upper-body gesture. Blender front and side previews and [a 1280 × 800 Godot dialogue render](images/rig-mara-standing-dialogue-1280x800.png) were inspected after export; a few small coat-panel gaps and detached trim flecks remain around the legs. A future art pass should refine their weights before treating this as a production character rig.

The Emerald Corsair's `Idle` and every `Walk` keyframe lower the arms and bring the boots closer together, so switching clips does not restore the source A-pose. The player-specific pose rotates each upper leg 14° inward and counter-rotates its shin 14°, placing each ankle below its knee; separate foot rotations turn the textured boots forward. Lateral pelvis sway is also reduced. The Blender build evaluates all 25 walk frames after skinning: the closest ankle stays `0.0452` source-model units from the centerline, the closest weighted boot vertex stays `0.0266` away, the largest lateral knee-to-ankle offset is `0.0127`, and textured boot headings stay within `-3.82°` to `+3.85°` of forward. The lowest boot remains at the source floor height within the build's `0.002` unit tolerance. Compare the raw 1280 × 800 macOS Godot OpenGL [before](images/rig-player-walk-before-frame-07-1280x800.png) and [after](images/rig-player-walk-after-frame-07-1280x800.png) captures of the same imported `Walk` frame and camera angle. The dock material changed between the two captures, so compare the character silhouette rather than the background. The earlier [player-input capture](images/rig-player-walk-camera-follow-1280x800.png) documents camera follow, while [the standing capture](images/rig-player-natural-stance-1280x800.png) shows the prior Idle silhouette. The in-place walk keys a small hip bob and reverse playback uses the same loop. The rear coat hem still needs weight refinement for close shots. These renders and geometry checks are desktop evidence, not a physical controller or Steam Deck test.

The clerk, vendor, and Ivo have character-specific arm and leg corrections in both `Idle` and `Talk`. Ivo's two arms need different angles to remain visible around his sculpted torso. The clerk and vendor's automatic weights initially attached parts of their long coats to forearm bones; the build script now transfers those stray lower-coat weights to the torso while keeping hand weights on the arms. Front, rear, and mid-gesture previews show the broad coat hems and split vendor tails intact. The exported NPC clips keep their lowest skinned point at the source floor within 0.1 mm in model space across sampled frames. Their poses remain first-pass animation, with small clerk trim flecks and some hand-to-coat overlap in close rear views.

An earlier Emerald Corsair probe resolved a bone-heat failure: Blender's default glTF import created 17,527 vertices in 2,124 disconnected components, and automatic weights assigned none. Importing with **Merge Vertices** produced 7,742 vertices in four components; the same 11-bone test assigned weights to all 7,742 vertices, and an upper-arm pose moved 2,276 evaluated vertices. The merged import retained its material, three images, and the UV corners of the surviving faces; one degenerate triangle collapsed. That probe established the import setting later used by the scripted rigs; it did not itself validate animation quality. Inspect textures and deformations in the game.

For either route, inspect the imported Godot scene for a weighted `MeshInstance3D`, `Skeleton3D`, skin binding, and actual bone tracks in `AnimationPlayer`. Verify loop mode and clip names, then play the dock scene to check foot contact, facing, and coat/limb deformation. Godot's [import documentation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.3/tutorials/assets_pipeline/importing_3d_scenes/import_configuration.html) explains the mesh–skeleton–skin binding. A skeleton node or an `Idle` name alone is insufficient evidence of a working rig.

`godot/tests/rig_slot_test.gd` builds both weighted and unweighted synthetic scenes to check clip selection, rejection of an unweighted skeleton, procedural motion, its foot anchor, and static fallback. The scene test checks the real rigged imports for bound skins, vertex weights, bone tracks, pose change, loop mode, controller heading, and camera follow. `scripts/verify-rigged-glbs.py` checks the exported GLB structure before import. None of these checks proves natural motion, coat clearance in every pose, or Steam Deck performance; inspect the graphical demo as well.
